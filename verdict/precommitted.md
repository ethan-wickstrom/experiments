# Where money is already on named problems (L2)

Checked 2026-11-26 against primary pages to see whether pre-committed demand can shortcut the buyer-pull loop today.

| Source | Verified state (2026-11-26) | Fits an afternoon loop? |
|---|---|---|
| **Algora** (`algora.io`) | Real, active escrowed bounties on open-source issues. Example: ZIO lists 49 open bounties totaling $23,245 ($123,425 awarded across 459 bounties); past fixed-spec challenges include Prettier ($25,000) and TSPerf ($15,000). | Fast and binding, but narrow: software engineering specs inside existing codebases, not domain-insider problems. |
| **US federal prize competitions** (`challenge.gov`, `nih.gov/challenges`) | `Challenge.gov` was sunset on March 30, 2026; active prizes moved to USA.gov and agency pages. NIH currently lists open challenges (COMMIT, NOURISH, INCLUDE Voices, Temporal Reasoning in Biomedical Knowledge Graphs). | Real money and domain problems, but months-long submission and judging cycles. |
| **New request/bounty boards** (`wantbuilt.com`, `elgora.ai`, `capeasy.co`) | Early or thin: WantBuilt shows 15 live requests and 1 live challenge; Elgora's homepage counters are blank; CapEasy lists 134 aggregated challenges behind a subscription. | Too thin or unverified to rely on as a primary channel. |

## Takeaway

Pre-committed money already exists, but splits cleanly into two shapes: fast engineering bounties (Algora) and slow institutional prizes (NIH / USA.gov). Neither replaces an afternoon test with ordinary people who live with a problem, so Verdict's main path stays direct asks to people who have already stated the problem (Runs 001 and 002).
