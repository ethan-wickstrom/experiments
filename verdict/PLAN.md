# The shortest schedule

## What can't be shortened

Two waits belong to the world. Everything else is agent time and takes hours.

1. **A person deciding.** Someone reads an ask and puts up money, time or a change to how they work. This takes an afternoon when they have already said they have the problem and know who is asking. Otherwise it has no upper bound.
2. **The outcome that lasts.** A second monthly payment, or use on day 42 that nobody prompted, arrives about six weeks after the first use. No amount of work moves it.

So the first full proof, an afternoon answer checked against a six-week verdict, takes about seven weeks from the first ask. A twelve-month plan is seven weeks of waiting and ten months of doing things one after another.

## How the rest is removed

1. **Start the slow clocks first.** The six-week wait starts on the day of the first real use, so the first real use comes before anything is polished.
2. **Run side by side.** Ten people in the same week, not ten weeks. In one trade, the people who named problems form one standing group that many fixes share, so no fix has to find its own. (The UK's RECOVERY trial got answers in 100 days this way: it made the trial part of routine care in 178 hospitals.)
3. **Write the guess down first.** Before every ask, the expected answer goes into `bets.jsonl` with a probability. In week seven the record shows how often the afternoon answer agreed with the six-week one. If it agrees often, later rounds act on the afternoon answer and stop waiting. (Netflix found decisions on 14 days of data agreed with day-63 results about 95% of the time, across 200 tests.) If it doesn't, the protocol is wrong and gets changed, six weeks in, not a year in.
4. **Begin where money is already on a named problem.** Prizes, bounties and tenders are buyers who stated a problem and committed money before any fix existed. The binding yes is already there. This is a lead, not yet checked.

## Gates, not dates

A step is passed when its evidence exists. Dates are the earliest the world allows.

| Gate | Evidence | Earliest |
|---|---|---|
| A | One binding answer, yes or no | The day a person is named |
| B | Ten binding answers in one week | Day 7 |
| C | Ten six-week verdicts, and the afternoon answers scored against them | Day 49 |
| D | One trade where every named problem has been tried by someone in it | Not known. It depends on how many people opt in, which is a bet to record, not a date to promise |

Past gate C the limit is trust: how many people will say what their problems are and answer an ask. Nobody can schedule that. It is earned one trade at a time, and it is lost at once if the asks turn into pitches.

## Who does what

| Part | Who |
|---|---|
| The plan, the protocol, the record and its scoring | Agent. Done |
| Turning a named problem into an ask and a working fix | Agent, within hours of a problem being named |
| Finding where money is already on named problems | Agent. Open as L2 |
| Naming the first people, or a trade to start in | Owner. Request R1 |
| Being the first person: one problem lived with for years | Owner. Request R2 |
| A way to take a deposit and return it | Owner. Request R3 |
| Contacting anyone | Owner, or the agent only after the owner says who and how. The agent sends no cold messages |
